# **Additive Exposure Logic: Tier 2 Earth-System and Hazard Nodes**

The characterization of Earth-system instability requires transitioning from static, historical hazard baselines to dynamic, non-linear exposure models. The Tier 2 logic framework isolates the unpriced vulnerabilities, physical boundaries, and systemic governance failures that precipitate compounding crises. Standard actuarial and infrastructure risk models predominantly evaluate direct, acute damage while severely discounting slow-onset ecosystem phase-shifts and chronic physiological wear. Consequently, systemic liabilities—ranging from the thermodynamic exhaustion of human labor to the structural failure of sub-polar foundations—remain hidden from sovereign risk assessments and capital allocation strategies1.  
By interrogating the specific threshold tolerances of planetary sinks, the sequence of burden distribution, and the rigidities in local infrastructure, this analysis highlights the structural mechanisms that sustain high exposure3. The following assessment synthesizes these parameters across major geophysical and ecological domains.
