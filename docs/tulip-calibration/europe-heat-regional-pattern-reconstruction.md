# Europe heat regional-pattern reconstruction

This validation asks whether TULIP can independently reproduce the regional temperature pattern behind the 2024 European anthropogenic heat-mortality study before using that pattern in a score.

The first phase uses the public 103-region Italian sample. It rebuilds regional weekly temperatures from daily ERA5-Land data and compares every available summer region-week from 2015 through 2022 with the authors' public sample. The spatial averaging method and daily timezone are selected once from this comparison and then locked. The historical 1950–2022 temperature series is regressed against the four-year HadCRUT5 global-temperature series only after those checks pass.

The validation is intentionally strict. All 103 regions must resolve; missing years cannot become zeros; every raw file is checksum-pinned; and the reconstruction must stay within the predeclared error tolerances in `internal-europe-heat-regional-pattern-ingestion.json`. Sardinia is the only non-direct region join. The public sample treats it as one region, so the pipeline dissolves the eight official NUTS 2010 Sardinian NUTS3 polygons into one declared aggregate.

The full candidate region universe is now reconstructed separately from official GISCO and Eurostat inputs: 823 unique regional units across the study's 35 countries, matching every published country count. All 823 units have a non-null historical Eurostat weekly-death record for 2019-W22. Thirty-four older region codes are absent from the equivalent 2022 slice because Albania, Belgium, Estonia and the United Kingdom changed regional boundaries or reporting codes. This is strong validation of the reconstructed universe, but it is not the authors' unpublished metadata table.

The official Eurostat population series cited by the article is also pinned for 2015–2022 and checked against a second Eurostat population collection. The two sources agree to within one person wherever both report a value. All 823 study-era region codes are covered in 2017–2019, but 22 old Belgian, Estonian and United Kingdom codes lack a boundary-aligned 2022 population. Carrying their nearest available values forward yields about 535.2 million people, roughly 7.8 million below the article's statement that the study covered more than 543 million people. That carry-forward is therefore retained only as a sensitivity case.

An official ONS workbook now supplies mid-2022 populations for all twelve UK slots. They sum to 67,596,281 and use the same NUTS1 geography, but their reference date is 30 June rather than Eurostat's 1 January. They remain a clearly labelled date-shifted sensitivity input. Eurostat's 2019 and 2022 LAU transmission workbooks then map 110 municipalities onto the ten changed Belgian and Estonian study regions. Eight regional values are direct old-boundary aggregates of the LAU 2022 edition population field. The workbook explicitly labels population and area as voluntary and not validated by Eurostat, and it does not state an exact reference date for the population field. These aggregates remain sensitivity inputs. BE221 and BE222 also share the later Oudsbergen municipality, so those two values retain one declared proportional split based on the two predecessor municipalities' 2019 populations.

An additional national benchmark sums Eurostat's official 1 January population series for the same 35 countries. Its highest total during 2015–2022 is 538,378,677 in 2020, which is still 4,621,323 below 543 million. The 2022 total is 538,272,629. Because even the national series does not reproduce the article's “over 543 million” statement, TULIP records this as a source-vintage or author-panel reconciliation question. It does not rescale the regional population panel to force agreement.

A separate secondary check reads the 2022 Opglabbeek and Oudsbergen totals displayed by AlleCijfers.be, which says its series comes from Statbel and uses 1 January population-register values. It reports 10,444 people in Opglabbeek and 23,611 in Oudsbergen, implying 13,167 in Meeuwen-Gruitrode. Compared with the declared proportional split, this would move about 50 people out of BE221 and 20 into BE222 and reduce the Europe-wide sensitivity total by 30. That shows the split is immaterial at the European scale, but the secondary publisher and unverified reuse terms mean these values remain corroboration only. A checksum-pinned direct Statbel file is still required before replacing the split.

Passing the Italian phase validates the temperature reconstruction method on the public sample. It does not recover the unpublished warming results for all 823 regions and does not by itself justify a TULIP rating. Full promotion still needs date-defined national-statistics values for the changed Belgian and Estonian boundaries, the Oudsbergen split replaced with a 2022 predecessor-area source, a documented UK date-alignment decision, reconciliation of the authors' population-panel vintage, a full-Europe reconstruction, reproduction of the published population-weighted warming check, and an independent calculation or source-provided regional slopes.

The exact CDS ERA5-Land daily-statistics download is account-gated. Copernicus's newer hourly ARCO route also requires a CDS API key. Google Earth Engine exposes a daily ERA5-Land aggregate, but requires an authenticated Earth Engine project and uses Google's daily aggregation, so it is retained as an independent cross-check rather than a silent replacement. The public Google ARCO bucket contains ERA5 rather than ERA5-Land and is rejected for this reconstruction. The repository prepares exact annual CDS requests but does not accept the Copernicus licence on a user's behalf. After the licence is accepted, run:

```sh
python3 -m pip install -r scripts/requirements-tulip-regional-pattern.txt
python3 scripts/fetch-europe-heat-regional-pattern-era5.py --stage validation
python3 scripts/reconstruct-europe-heat-regional-pattern.py --stage validation
```

The historical retrieval remains separate until the validation chooses and locks the aggregation settings.
