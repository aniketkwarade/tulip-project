# Displacement life-year route: source reproduction check

Prepared 27 September 2026. This is an experimental human-consequence route check, not a new TULIP rating.

The [IDMC GRID 2026 report](https://api.internal-displacement.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/IDMC-GRID-2026-Global-Report-on-Internal-Displacement.pdf) distinguishes **13.6 million people still displaced by disasters at the end of 2025** from **29.9 million disaster-related internal movements during 2025**. The stock counts people at one point in time; the flow counts moves and can include repeated moves by the same person. Neither figure is restricted to climate-attributed displacement. The current [Migration review](../../artifacts/tulip-calibration-2026-09-22/migration-review.md) already keeps these quantities separate. Neither can supply a duration or a global welfare-loss total by itself.

IDMC's [July 2026 methodological note](https://api.internal-displacement.org/sites/default/files/publications/documents/2026_IDMC_A%20life-year%20approach%20for%20measuring%20the%20human%20cost%20of%20disaster%20displacement.pdf) offers a more useful consequence route: displacement-related *wellbeing life-years lost* = `e × T × N`, with a stated average wellbeing reduction `e = 0.042`, affected people `N`, and disruption years `T = observed evacuation days × recovery factor ÷ 365`. The recovery factor uses four classified event characteristics. This is a welfare measure inspired by DALYs, **not a WHO DALY**; adding it to the WHO health-burden endpoint would require an explicit overlap and equivalence decision.

Before adopting that calculation, [the reproduction audit](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/idmc-displacement-life-year-reproduction.json) applies it to the five rounded worked examples in IDMC's [Bangladesh report, Table 1](https://api.internal-displacement.org/sites/default/files/publications/documents/2026_IDMC_The%20human%20cost%20of%20disaster%20displacement%20in%20Bangladesh.pdf). The general note's `e = 0.042` falls below all five printed life-year totals beyond a simple displayed-precision allowance. The Bangladesh report, however, says it used the *upper bound* of a mild-anxiety disability-weight range without printing the value. A [published GBD research estimate](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10017349/) gives one such upper uncertainty bound as **0.046**. Substituting `e = 0.046` makes all five results consistent with the printed totals if movement counts are rounded to 0.1 million and disruption days to whole days. That is a plausible reconciliation, **not proof** of the exact weight, source, rounding rules or event inputs used by IDMC. Those illustrative allowances are not statistical uncertainty intervals. In particular, the GBD paper is not a source attribution for the Bangladesh report's wording about WHO.

The same audit tests the recovery-factor threshold. With the affected group and observed evacuation duration held fixed, moving the source's four-part event classification from total **7** to **8** changes its recovery factor from **4** to **7**, raising calculated loss by **75%**. That is a published modeling choice, not an empirical discontinuity in harm. A TULIP transfer would need sensitivity bounds around such classification boundaries and independent review of the event categories.

The route therefore remains **unadmitted** for TULIP scoring. The arithmetic mismatch has a credible explanation, but the exact application parameter is still unconfirmed. A reproducible application needs the event inputs or clarification of that parameter; a consistent definition of unique affected people versus movements; duration and recovery data; a climate-attribution boundary for climate nodes; and a rule preventing overlap with health and other service losses. Once those exist, a frozen mapping from welfare life-years to the people component can be tested on held-out events and future nodes. The Bangladesh 2008–2025 cumulative estimate cannot be relabeled as the current global Migration score.
