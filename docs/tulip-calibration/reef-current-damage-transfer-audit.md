# Coral-bleaching damage transfer audit

Prepared 27 September 2026. This is a route decision for an experimental score, not a change to public ratings.

The 2023–2024 [GCRMN global report](https://gcrmn.net/2026/08/31/gcrmn-2025-report/) records an approximately 8.9% fall in modeled global hard-coral cover following the fourth bleaching event. The [source-bound TULIP observation](../../src/tulip-reef-event-observation.js) reproduces the annual 28.29% to 25.77% cover change. It is a biological outcome measurement, but not a no-bleaching counterfactual: other pressures operated during the same year, and the report supplies no numerical bleaching-only fraction. The 2023 cover value therefore must not be entered as the modeled no-bleaching condition for 2024.

The [2014–2017 global study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67506-w) estimates that 51% of world reefs experienced moderate-or-greater bleaching and 15% moderate-or-greater coral mortality during that *earlier event*. These are modeled fractions of **reef locations with an outcome**, not fractions of reef area or coral cover lost. The study fitted mortality probability against satellite degree-heating weeks, with interactions among heat stress, ocean basin **and event year**. Its mortality response was less well constrained than its bleaching response, and it found no positive heat-stress/mortality relationship in the Caribbean/Atlantic during that event. Its reported global result came from applying fitted probabilities to reef-containing pixels, not multiplying one worldwide exposure percentage by one worldwide mortality percentage.

[NOAA's fourth-event status](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/satellite/research/coral_bleaching_report.php) reports bleaching-level **heat stress** over about 84.4% of global reef area from January 2023 through September 2025. That percentage is not a measured bleaching or mortality fraction. Multiplying 84.4% by the older study's 15% would combine different events, denominators and outcomes. Nor can the older fitted response be run unchanged for 2023–2025: it needs the current pixel-level heat-stress distribution and reef mask, an explicit rule for the model's unseen event-year terms, current field validation by basin, and propagated model uncertainty. An arbitrary choice of the 2014, 2015 or 2016 coefficient would make the answer depend on TULIP's convenience rather than evidence.

The transfer decision is **not admitted**. A defensible next route is to obtain current field bleaching/mortality observations matched to NOAA 5-km heat-stress pixels, fit or update an event-specific conditional response model, validate it out of sample by basin and year, then integrate over a compatible reef-area distribution. The model should estimate a no-bleaching counterfactual or otherwise apportion concurrent causes, and keep coral-cover loss, prevalence of bleaching, prevalence of mortality and recovery time as separate outcomes. If those data cannot be obtained, GCRMN's event-associated cover decline remains an outcome context and a sensitivity bound, not a numeric node-attributable harm component.

The [nature vector](../../src/tulip-nature-burden-experiment.js) now requires explicit modeled no-node and with-node condition fractions, rather than an assessor-entered ambiguous “percent loss.” Its tests use synthetic pairs; the 2023/2024 observed GCRMN pair does **not** pass as a causal pair. Even a valid reef-specific burden would still need the cross-ecosystem calibration and human-domain applicability decision in the [future-node acceptance contract](./future-node-acceptance-contract.md) before a full TULIP Score.
