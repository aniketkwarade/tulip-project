# Nature-side common-unit feasibility check

Prepared 27 September 2026. **Method research only; public scores unchanged.**

The nature component needs a stable meaning before assigning 1–10 anchors. A forest conversion rate, coral-cover decline, species extinction risk and freshwater service disruption are different observations. Their numerical units cannot be merged by a curve applied after the fact.

[UN SEEA Ecosystem Accounting](https://seea.un.org/en/methodology/ecosystem-accounting) separates ecosystem **extent** (area by type) from **condition** (measured characteristics relative to a reference). That is a useful current-state accounting structure, but it does not declare one universal hectare or one condition percentage equivalent across every ecosystem type. [IUCN's Red List of Ecosystems](https://iucn.org/resources/conservation-tool/iucn-red-list-ecosystems) standardizes assessment of **collapse risk** from distribution and degradation evidence; a risk category is not a measurement of current attributable harm.

[LC-IMPACT](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1111/jiec.13018) offers a more explicit modeled common endpoint for some pressures: the potentially disappeared fraction of species over time, or **PDF·year**. Its authors say this represents modeled global extinction *risk* associated with an exposure period, not observed species extinctions at a date. They recommend reporting terrestrial, freshwater and marine contributions separately because no generally accepted weighting between those ecosystems exists. Using PDF·year as TULIP's sole nature unit would silently change the public construct from **current harm** to a particular modeled biodiversity-risk construct, and it would omit ecosystem functions and service losses outside that model.

The [LC-IMPACT V2 land-use factors](https://zenodo.org/records/19202729) supply a concrete compatibility challenge. The [reproducible audit](../../scripts/audit-lc-impact-forest-conversion-route.py) checks the downloaded land-use archive and selects the same `from forest, natural` transformation flow, aggregated terrestrial species, average approach, for four countries. The modeled factor for Indonesia is **4,338 times** the factor for Finland for that named flow. This is a difference between characterization factors, **not a measured 4,338-fold difference in actual forest harm**. The country table includes both occupation (`Global PDF/m²`) and transformation (`Global PDF·year/m²`) factors, as well as 1,786 negative and 268 missing factor rows; none should be silently treated as zero or positive danger.

Our [FAO forest endpoint](./source-native-ecosystem-extent-pilot.md) is **10.9 million hectares per year globally, averaged over 2015–2025**. It lacks the country or ecoregion distribution, origin-to-destination land-use flows and transformation/occupation accounting that LC-IMPACT's factors require. Multiplying FAO's total by one selected factor would invent precision and ecological meaning. The [audit artifact](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/lc-impact-forest-conversion-route-audit.json) therefore records **no nature score**.

For TULIP's current-harm goal, the workable next unit is **type-specific condition loss over a measured extent and period**. A complete nature packet must name the ecosystem type and fixed distribution; its reference and current condition; the affected area or population; the attribution and counterfactual; and observed persistence or a defensible recovery model. A modeled PDF·year result can be a separate biodiversity endpoint when its inventory, geography, model version and assumptions match. Neither endpoint becomes a cross-ecosystem 1–10 score until TULIP explicitly chooses and tests the value trade-offs among habitat types, biodiversity, functions and recovery. The health component's WHO DALY anchors cannot supply those trade-offs.

The next falsification cases should compare a severe small loss in a rare habitat with a mild widespread loss in a common habitat, and compare two pressures on the **same** ecosystem with identical extent but different measured condition. Use held-out real examples after the cross-type rule is frozen. New nature nodes must reuse that rule and its source contracts; adding a node must not move existing anchors.

## Cross-type denominators are a policy choice

The [UN SEEA guidance on ecosystem condition, section 5.87](https://seea.un.org/sites/seea.un.org/files/documents/EA/seea_ea_f124_web_12dec24.pdf) cautions against aggregating condition across different realms, such as marine and terrestrial, or across different reference conditions. The [IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology](https://iucn.org/resources/conservation-tool/iucn-global-ecosystem-typology) offers a versioned hierarchy of ecosystem types, but it does not supply exchange rates between a lost reef-condition unit and a lost forest-condition unit. TULIP can define such a weighting as policy; it cannot present it as an ecological constant.

The [denominator sensitivity experiment](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/nature-denominator-sensitivity.json) uses source-reported **areas only**—GCRMN's mapped reef area and FAO's 4.14-billion-hectare global forest extent—with explicitly **hypothetical** condition losses over the same duration. A 10% reef loss exceeds a 1% forest loss by **10×** on a share-of-type ruler, but the forest example exceeds the reef example by **15.9×** on a simple condition-adjusted-area ruler. The ranking reverses without changing the observations. These are not measured TULIP cases; they expose the hidden value choice in the denominator.

An unweighted sum of type-relative loss has a second defect: splitting one forest type into two equal subtypes with identical harm **doubles** its summed value. A mean over currently listed types also changes when an unrelated zero-loss type is added. The eventual nature method must freeze a hierarchy and a weight-inheritance rule so taxonomy refinements and unrelated future nodes cannot alter old assessments. A compatible condition reference, causal attribution and recovery period are still required for each real node. The [executable test](../../scripts/analyze-tulip-nature-denominator-sensitivity.mjs) preserves these invariants as rejection cases for future candidates.

## A measured condition case still is not a TULIP nature score

The [GCRMN 2025 global reef assessment](https://gcrmn.net/2025-report/) gives a usable example of a source-native **condition** indicator. Its [pinned source extraction](../../artifacts/tulip-calibration-2026-09-22/coral-bleaching-reference-snapshot.json) reports mean modeled live hard-coral cover of **30.212667%** for 1980–2009 and **27.354%** for 2020–2024. The relative change, `(27.354 / 30.212667 - 1) × 100`, is **−9.46%**. The 2024 model estimate is 25.77% cover, with a reported 95% interval of 24.64–27.01%; it is a different, single-year comparison. The period-mean relative decline is a reproducible *reef-condition observation*, not a statement that 9.46% of reefs were destroyed or that 9.46% of coral bleaching damage was caused by climate change.

GCRMN also reports reef-area weights and change estimates for subregions. In the extracted table, subregions with modeled decline represent 37.80% of mapped reef area, but change is unavailable for subregions representing 46.92%. Neither percentage counts individual damaged reefs, and the subregion change percentages cannot be multiplied by mapped area to obtain area of coral lost without compatible baseline cover for each subregion. Heat-stress exposure from NOAA is a different endpoint again.

This case passes a narrow **same-indicator, same-source, declared-period** reproducibility check. It does **not** pass whole-node numerical admission: hard-coral cover alone omits other reef condition dimensions; the global trend is not apportioned to the `coral_bleaching` node's attributable share; and no frozen trade-off says how a reef-cover decline compares with forest conversion or freshwater damage. A future reef node could reuse the same condition definition and source protocol, but its score would require a node-specific attributable consequence and the same fixed cross-ecosystem calibration. Adding that node must not reset the reef reference period or any TULIP anchor.

The [experimental condition evaluator](../../src/tulip-ecosystem-condition-experiment.js) now binds those two period means to an independently registered, digest-pinned GCRMN extraction. Its [challenge tests](../../scripts/test-tulip-ecosystem-condition-experiment.mjs) reject a changed source value, geography, unit, period, unsupported interval, bleaching-exposure substitution or assertion that the trend is already node-attributable. Reusing the observation under a synthetic future node ID leaves its value unchanged and still assigns **no score**. This is a source-input gate, not the missing cross-ecosystem calibration.

The [subregional condition × extent audit](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/gcrmn-reef-cover-area-audit.json) tests whether we can retain a physical magnitude before the nature display scale. It downloads all ten checksum-pinned GCRMN regional tables, joins their paired period cover to the report's mapped reef areas, and reports **cover-equivalent square kilometres**: `reef area × (reference cover − current cover)`. This is a change in modeled live-coral surface cover over mapped reefs, **not** square kilometres of destroyed reef, a biodiversity-equivalence unit, or climate-attributable damage. The [script](../../scripts/analyze-gcrmn-reef-cover-area.mjs) and [offline arithmetic test](../../scripts/test-gcrmn-reef-cover-area-audit.mjs) reproduce the calculation.

| Exploratory calculation | Result | Interpretation limit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Paired reference/current cover | 47 of 51 subregions; 98.05% of mapped reef area | A point estimate exists even where change is not well established. |
| Gross decline across all paired modeled subregions | 10,873 km² cover-equivalent | Includes model point declines without the report's change-evidence threshold. |
| Gross decline in 18 subregions with at least one change-evidence bar | 9,227 km² cover-equivalent | Covers 37.80% of mapped reef area; neither a global total nor a statistical lower bound. |
| Net difference across paired subregions | 6,322 km² cover-equivalent | Gains elsewhere offset losses and four subregions lack paired cover. |
| Global mean difference × mapped reef area | 7,445 km² cover-equivalent | A different aggregate of the hierarchical model; it must not be substituted for gross spatial loss. |

The report withholds a supported change classification over **46.92%** of mapped reef area despite providing modeled period values for much of it. Thus a formula that simply multiplies every point estimate by area would promote weak evidence into measured harm; a formula that uses only the 18 supported declines would silently omit unresolved regions. The next nature assessment needs a compatible attribution model, posterior uncertainty for the spatial aggregate, recovery/persistence, and a declared cross-ecosystem reference before this could enter a TULIP component. These are specific missing inputs for the coral case, not a reason to treat its area-equivalent quantity as a score now.

The same [GCRMN report](https://gcrmn.net/2026/08/31/gcrmn-2025-report/) describes an approximately **8.9% global hard-coral-cover decline following the 2023–2024 bleaching event**. Its modelled annual series gives 28.29% cover in 2023 and 25.77% in 2024: a 2.52 percentage-point fall. Multiplying that *global modeled cover difference* by the report's mapped reef area gives **6,563 km² of cover-equivalent change**. The [source-bound evaluator](../../src/tulip-reef-event-observation.js) and [test](../../scripts/test-tulip-reef-event-observation.mjs) reproduce this narrower current observation from the pinned snapshot. It is not 6,563 km² of reef destroyed, nor a bleaching-only causal estimate: the report associates the decline with the event but does not provide a numeric apportionment among bleaching and other simultaneous pressures. The 8.91% calculated from the two annual point estimates is not necessarily the median change from paired posterior draws. GCRMN's [analysis code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GCRMN/global_2025/7ef705f7009f51102582bbb15a88026fce140ed2/code/d07_benthic-cover_trends.R) computes event contrasts from paired draws, but the separate 2023 and 2024 marginal intervals in our pinned snapshot cannot be subtracted to claim an uncertainty interval for their paired change. Duration and other reef functions are also unmeasured here. This improves the current condition input for `coral_bleaching` while leaving its whole-node nature component and score unadmitted.

## Fixed-type arithmetic gate for future nodes

The [experimental nature-vector evaluator](../../src/tulip-nature-burden-experiment.js) now preserves a reviewed cohort's `affected area × condition-loss fraction × duration` **within its named indicator and ecosystem type**. It has two fixed, source-snapshot-pinned extent denominators: GCRMN's 260,433.73766 km² of mapped reef and Global Mangrove Watch v4.1.12's 147,283.6426 km² of mapped mangrove extent in 2025. These are denominator references, not claims that their condition indicators are equivalent. Live hard-coral cover and mangrove extent are different measures; the evaluator returns a typed vector and no combined number or TULIP Score.

The [synthetic challenge test](../../scripts/test-tulip-nature-burden-experiment.mjs) checks the snapshot digests and area conversions, then tests that splitting a declared disjoint cohort or adding an unrelated node does not change the burden for the original type. Hectares and equivalent square kilometres give identical results. It also tests within-type monotonicity and rejects unknown units, changed condition metrics, repeated spatial IDs, excess affected area, unmatched periods and absent attribution records. These are **arithmetic and schema tests**, not evidence that the hypothetical cohorts occurred or were caused by any TULIP node. A spatial ID or assessor-entered model name cannot establish non-overlap or causal attribution; both still require independent source/model review. The mangrove source maps extent and gain/loss, not the cause of each loss. No real node has been admitted by this evaluator.

The remaining decision is substantive: choose and independently challenge a rule for comparing different kinds of ecosystem harm, then freeze its reference states, type hierarchy and weights. A formula over the typed vector cannot make those ecological trade-offs disappear. After that rule is set, real node-bound cohorts must be sourced and tested before any nature rating is published.

The [reef transfer audit](./reef-current-damage-transfer-audit.md) tests whether the 2014–2017 global bleaching/mortality model and NOAA's newer heat-stress coverage can supply such a cohort for the fourth event. They cannot without an event-specific response and compatible spatial inputs: the older model has basin-by-year response interactions, while NOAA's headline percentage measures heat exposure rather than biological damage. The vector's input has consequently been tightened to derive absolute condition loss from a stated no-node counterfactual and with-node condition pair. A prior year's observed cover is not automatically that counterfactual.

The [ecosystem severity normalization candidate](./ecosystem-severity-normalization-candidate.md) now tests IUCN's source-defined reference-to-collapse indicator scale. It reproduces the worked input arithmetic and preserves one small printed percentage discrepancy. Matched current with-node/without-node scenarios can use the same scale for an experimental incremental indicator difference. This can replace a freely entered ordinal severity for compatible continuous indicators; it does not supply reef collapse thresholds, causal attribution, complete ecological condition, or a cross-type score. Frequency-based or nonmonotone indicators still need separate routes.

The [fixed-policy aggregation challenge](./nature-aggregation-policy-challenge.md) now implements an inherited canonical-type denominator and separately registered weights on a synthetic two-family universe. Both equal-family and fixed-area policies preserve same-type magnitude, subtype partition and catalogue-addition invariance; neither drops an unsupported family. They reverse a hypothetical reef/mangrove comparison, so their mechanical stability cannot select a cross-type ecological exchange rate. Current damaged stock is not multiplied by history length or projected recovery in this test. All outputs remain experimental with no numerical TULIP rating.

The [coral community challenge and outcome coverage rule](./ecological-outcome-coverage-rule.md) tests another part of the missing calibration: whether one condition measure represents the whole ecological outcome. It retains 262 historical site/community pairs, including unchanged cover with replacement among categories. Total cover and category covers overlap, and a positive category-change sum varies with the category partition. This rules out treating either net cover alone or an uncalibrated sum of category decreases as a universal nature-harm unit. The source pool supplies real measurement cases for endpoint review; it does not select ecological trade-offs or complete current node assessments.
