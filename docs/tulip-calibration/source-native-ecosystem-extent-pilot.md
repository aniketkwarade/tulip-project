# Source-native ecosystem extent pilot: deforestation

Prepared 27 September 2026. This is a nature-side input test, **not a TULIP rating**.

[FAO's Global Forest Resources Assessment 2025 release](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/global-deforestation-slows--but-forests-remain-under-pressure--fao-report-shows/en) reports global **gross deforestation averaging 10.9 million hectares per year over 2015–2025**. It separately reports **4.12 million hectares per year of net forest loss** and **6.78 million hectares per year of forest expansion**. Deforestation is forest conversion to another land use; a newly expanded forest does not erase the ecological consequences at a different converted site. The [source transcription](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/fao-fra-2025-global-deforestation-extent.json) retains FAO's one-decimal-million-hectare resolution and records that the ten-year average is **not an independently observed 2025 value**.

The [experimental endpoint validator](../../src/tulip-ecosystem-extent-experiment.js) accepts the gross area-conversion rate only with its geography, ecosystem definition, unit and exact 2015–2025 averaging period. It rejects substitution of the net-loss or generic tree-cover-loss rate, a different period, a changed unit, invented precision, an unsupported uncertainty interval and a claim of whole-node coverage. It now also checks the candidate's copied observation against a separate, versioned [FAO source registration](../../src/tulip-source-observation-registry.js). The [tests](../../scripts/test-tulip-ecosystem-extent-experiment.mjs) verify the transcription artifact's digest and reject a changed FAO value even when the copied observation remains internally consistent. Adding an unrelated node does not alter the source-native result. Registration prevents a packet from declaring its own number to be sourced; it does not independently validate forest condition or a complete TULIP component.

The result is **10.9 million hectares per year of forest-to-other-land-use conversion**, not a measured nature-harm score. FAO's global summary does not say how much ecological condition was lost per converted hectare, distinguish the full value of forest types, establish recovery time for each conversion, or provide a numerical uncertainty interval for this rate. Multiplying the rounded average by ten would estimate a period total, not the currently damaged forest stock or its recovery history. Nor does it establish the human consequences attributable to the conversion. The catalogue marks Deforestation as a people-and-nature node, so this one endpoint cannot fill either the complete nature component or the whole node.

This is a useful failure of a tempting shortcut: dividing the rate by global forest area would produce a percent-of-area statistic, but it would not measure lost ecosystem function, biodiversity or persistence. Subtracting forest expansion would answer a different question. Before conversion to 1–10, a nature route needs source-matched condition and duration evidence, a fixed ecosystem reference, and an explicit overlap rule for related nodes such as fragmentation and species loss. Those choices must be calibrated on independent cases rather than fitted to the current catalogue.

A [follow-up common-unit audit](./nature-common-unit-feasibility.md) checked whether LC-IMPACT V2's modeled species-loss-over-time factors could supply the missing conversion. The source factors depend strongly on location and land-use flow; FAO's global aggregate lacks both. No nature component or score was produced by that check.
