# Mixed-case calibration decision and catalogue gate

Prepared 27 September 2026. Status: **working internal experiment; current public ratings unchanged**.

## Decision

The replacement score's construct is **aggregate current attributable consequence burden**. Reject the **equal-weight geometric consequence index** as its scoring rule. Keep it as an experimental comparator only, applied to people and nature **separately** before the 50/50 average where both domains apply. The aggregate-burden and [anchor-saturation tests](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/anchor-saturation.json) below show that its ordinal inputs cannot preserve measured harm magnitude across the intended decision range. Do not assign a special seriousness premium or tune a final curve to conceal this input problem.

For an applicable domain whose seriousness, affected reach and consequence duration each have a justified 0–4 level:

`index = cube_root((seriousness/4) × (affected reach/4) × (consequence duration/4))`

The piecewise map sends verified all-zero harm to 1.0; 1/1/1 to 3.0; 2/2/2 to 5.0; 3/3/3 to 7.0; 4/3/4 to 9.0; and 4/4/4 to 10.0. Mixed zero/positive axes are rejected. The [versioned evaluator](../../src/tulip-consequence-scoring-v2.js) enforces the same named outcome, affected group, place, period, attribution, overlap and joint-bound checks as the earlier trial.

This choice is a **TULIP calibration convention**. A geometric combination stops a very large value on one axis from fully offsetting a small value on another. Equal exponents avoid an extra unsupported preference for seriousness, reach or duration. The benchmark map stays an experiment: treating ordinal 0–4 levels as numbers in a product is itself a value choice until the levels are calibrated against measured consequences.

## Mixed cases judged before the new catalogue run

The [case file](./mixed-case-judgments.json) records four directional judgments and four pairs deliberately left unresolved. Its hypothetical examples describe attributable harm, not exposure. It was written after the earlier additive trial was known, but before the geometric catalogue comparison. It is an internal challenge set, not a blind external validation.

| Candidate | Required judgments passed | Large serious case | Small permanent case | Widespread mild case |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Equal arithmetic | 1 / 4 | 7.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 |
| Arithmetic 40 / 30 / 30 | 2 / 4 | 7.0 | 7.3 | 6.6 |
| Arithmetic 50 / 25 / 25 | 2 / 4 | 7.0 | 7.7 | 6.0 |
| **Equal geometric** | **4 / 4** | **7.0** | **6.0** | **6.0** |
| Geometric 40 / 30 / 30 | 4 / 4 | 7.0 | 6.3 | 5.6 |

The table's three profiles use the same levels in either domain; their **human and ecosystem interpretations differ**. For people, 10 million serious cases lasting five years should exceed 5,000 permanent-disability cases under the stated example. For the same ecosystem type, major condition loss over 20% of its distribution should exceed very severe loss over only 0.5% when that patch has no specified unique irreplaceability, within a declared **50-year comparison horizon**. A much longer horizon could change that judgment. This horizon adjudicates only the hypothetical ranking test; it does not add a future forecast to a current node score. The comparison deliberately leaves the small permanent and widespread mild cases tied under equal geometric weighting; their ordering needs further evidence about health burden or ecological irreplaceability. The [full comparison](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/mixed-case-comparison.json) also shows how each candidate changes existing catalogue values **if old axis judgments are reused**. Those conditional changes are not new ratings.

## Aggregate-burden interpretation gate

The [human-burden stress case](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/human-burden-stress.json) exposes a different trade-off the four judged pairs did not settle. In an **invented** example, 100 million people have mild symptoms for fourteen days, while 5,000 people have a permanent disability for forty years. The geometric rule gives them **4.2** and **6.0**, respectively. If the example uses illustrative disability weights of 0.05 and 0.5, the first has about **192,000** healthy years lost and the second **100,000**. WHO's [years-lived-with-disability method](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/gho-documents/global-health-estimates/ghe2019_daly-methods.pdf) uses cases × duration × disability weight; these particular weights and cases are TULIP stress assumptions, not WHO estimates. At a severe-case weight of 0.5, the mild weight needs to exceed about **0.026** for the aggregate ordering to reverse the geometric ordering.

This does not prove one ordering is morally correct. It tests the consequences of the chosen **aggregate-burden** construct. A coarse noncompensatory ordinal product can disagree with a compatible health-burden estimate. A final 1–10 transformation cannot restore magnitude lost when counts and durations were first compressed into 0–4 levels. The geometric rule is therefore ruled out as the replacement aggregate-harm method, even though it passed the four earlier directional judgments. Those judgments remain useful challenges for the next candidate and may require source-native magnitude or explicit, separately disclosed policy trade-offs rather than a hidden seriousness premium.

The [saturation diagnostic](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/anchor-saturation.json) removes even the need to compare unlike severity classes: two invented mild cases with the *same* disability weight and duration differ tenfold in harmed people, yet both score 4.2 once the 100-million-person reach level caps. With reach held fixed, ten versus forty years of the same harm both score 6.0 after the duration level caps. A different final power curve cannot distinguish either pair. If aggregate current harm is the agreed construct, keep the source-native quantities through the burden calculation and calibrate the 1–10 display afterward against fixed evidence-backed anchors.

As a narrow mechanism check, the [fixed WHO-based health-anchor candidate](./human-health-anchor-candidate.md) maps the same **invented** 192,000 versus 100,000 health-years-lost cases to illustrative health-only readings of **3.6** versus **3.0**. That preserves the aggregate ordering that the geometric 4.2 versus 6.0 reverses. It does not validate the illustrative disability weights, establish that both cases share a compatible incident-year timebase in real data, score a node, or settle people-versus-nature equivalence. It does show which stage must retain magnitude: the source-native consequence calculation, before display mapping.

## What the evidence review found

The [review ledger](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/review-ledger.csv) now has one decision row for every **354 issue nodes and 27 response nodes**, with **518 applicable people/nature components** in the [full evidence record](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/review-ledger.json). Every issue has a typed v2 draft with original axes and source references preserved. No draft is marked validated merely because its old axes produce a number.

The [original 20-node semantic screen](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/original-20-semantic-review.json) reviews each pilot record's stored endpoint rationale. It finds partly usable routes for modeled air-pollution mortality, coral-cover decline and a city water-restriction episode, but none of the 18 issue records yet meets the complete same-outcome, geography, period and joint-bound contract. The [six primary-source boundary findings](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/priority-source-findings.json) check the highest-risk substitutions against their source pages. Across all 1,554 old axis flags, 84 are marked directly source-anchored, 1,008 are explicitly structured rather than source-anchored, and 462 have no such flag. These labels alone do not decide validity, but they show why a blanket numerical conversion would overclaim the evidence.

- **2 central components fail**: Glacier-Fed Water Dependence and Surface Water Storage Instability. Their new central axes are withheld. The [node-specific correction decisions](./central-consequence-repair-decisions.md) identify the exact incompatible inputs and acceptable replacement evidence. The former source measures dependence on mountain water, and the latter reports people living in basins with receding permanent water. Neither count measures a harmed group or the duration of its harm. A regional 2026 meltwater model estimates people whose scarcity was **alleviated** by meltwater; it cannot be substituted for people harmed by global meltwater dependence. See [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/reports/wwdr/2025), [UN-Water](https://www.unwater.org/publications/progress-water-related-ecosystems-2024-update) and the [regional model](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73245-3).
- **132 lower components on 110 nodes fail as joint scenarios**. For 76, seriousness is zero while other axes remain positive; the reviewer must decide whether no harm is genuinely possible. For 37, positive seriousness is paired with zero affected reach. For 19, positive seriousness and reach are paired with zero consequence duration. The [per-component repair docket](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/lower-bound-repair-queue.csv) retains each old tuple, its axis rationale, source links and the exact decision needed. The draft lower bound is withheld for each; the original is kept for audit. No blanket zero or positive replacement has been inserted.
- **The remaining 352 central vectors are only mathematically calculable.** Their sources must still show what outcome happened, who or what experienced it, the comparable place and period, the attribution, and how seriousness was summarized for that affected group. There are **zero completed typed outcome assessments** in this pass. The 27 response nodes still have no danger rating.

An example of why this matters beyond the two zero failures: the current Air Pollution Health Burden record uses WHO's modeled death count for its central people reach, but its upper reach comes from the 99% of people living where air quality exceeds WHO guideline levels. Those are different affected groups. I fetched the [WHO AIR_35 global 2021 row](https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/AIR_35?%24filter=SpatialDim+eq+%27GLOBAL%27+and+TimeDim+eq+2021+and+Dim1+eq+%27SEX_BTSX%27+and+Dim2+eq+%27GHECAUSE_GHE000000%27&%24format=json): it reports 6.574 million modeled joint ambient-and-household attributable deaths, with a source interval of 4.937–8.360 million. The [mortality-only reach review](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/air-pollution-mortality-reach-review.json) maps those three death counts to the same affected-people scale and excludes the exposure statistic. This repairs **one endpoint's reach bound**, not the full node: the severity, enduring loss and possible nonfatal burden still need an overlap-safe typed assessment. The public 9.3 is unchanged.

A second source-bound failure is the global Heat-Attributable Excess Mortality node. Its central death count comes from a [35-country European summer-2022 attribution study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-024-00783-2), while its upper reach invokes a separate global heat-mortality series. The studies answer different geographic and causal questions. The new direct-source scope check rejects a regional or exposure observation if its declared endpoint, affected group, place or assessment period does not match the assessment. A regional case can be assessed as regional; it cannot silently become the uncertainty bound of a global case.

## Release gate for the new method

Before a v2 value can replace a public v6 value, its typed assessment must pass the evaluator, its cited source must actually support the recorded outcome and affected group, and its lower/central/upper cases must each have a named, same-outcome joint scenario. The evaluator now rejects a bound without that joint evidence, even when its three axis levels are individually in range. A direct estimate must also carry a numeric source observation with the same endpoint, affected group, geography and period; an attributable-death case must bind all three harmed-person counts to its one source interval. For a **mortality-only** endpoint it also requires a complete-endpoint declaration and the TULIP policy levels for death and irreversible consequence; the same WHO death count can no longer acquire a different experimental rating merely by entering milder levels. The [direct-mortality diagnostic](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/direct-mortality-identifiability.json) records this targeted correction. A mortality-only calculation does **not** validate the full Air Pollution Health Burden node or any unmeasured nonfatal effects. This is a structural gate; typed fields do not prove the scientific claims, so a second internal check must reproduce the source extraction, classification and node coverage. Quantitative counts or percentages can remove unnecessary ties only after the geography, denominator, period and outcome match. Scores must remain present for all issue nodes when a full replacement is released, so the existing public scores stay in place while these records are rebuilt. This decision does not install a phone build or publish a release.

The [independent Python calculation](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/geometric-verification.json) reproduces all 352 conditional values, rejects both invalid central records and confirms all 27 response exclusions. It verifies arithmetic rather than scientific meaning; the construct failure above still rules out those conditional values as replacement scores.
