# Nature weighting: the provisional internal choice

27 September 2026. **Policy decision for the experimental method; no public score changes.**

Use **fixed physical area** to weight comparable current-condition losses. This follows the agreed aggregate-current-harm construct: the same amount of comparable loss should count equally rather than receive an implicit bonus because it occurs in a smaller ecosystem family. This choice is provisional while the user preference is pending. It does not establish ecological equivalence or approve global nature calibration.

For the experiment's frozen, compatible family group:

`index = sum(disjoint affected area × attributable condition degradation) / fixed total group area`

This is algebraically identical to weighting each family's loss fraction by its share of the fixed group area. Family denominators cancel in that aggregate; they remain useful for showing how much of each type is affected. The [decision record](./nature-aggregation-selection.json) stores the rule, assumptions and limits separately from node assessments.

## Why this choice, and its downside

Equal-family weighting answers a different question: how much of each equally valued ecosystem family has been lost? It deliberately gives a unit of loss in a small family more influence than the same comparable loss in a large one. That can suit a conservation-diversity objective. TULIP has chosen total current consequence burden as its experimental headline construct, so this premium must not enter unnoticed.

Area weighting has its own assumption: the condition scale must make the compared losses commensurable. One square kilometre of lost coral cover is not automatically equivalent to one square kilometre of removed mangrove forest. Reference-to-collapse normalization alone does not prove that exchange. The current toy condition indicators were invented to test this arithmetic; they are not source observations of whole ecological function.

The rule can underrepresent unique, small ecosystems. Rarity, irreplaceability, connectivity or permanent loss may warrant a different explicitly calibrated consequence rule. Area is not evidence for their importance, and an unexplained bonus would obscure rather than settle the trade-off. Preserve these characteristics in the outcome profile and explanation.

## Exact implication of the existing experiment

The source-pinned mapped references in the [prior experiment](./nature-aggregation-policy-challenge.md) are approximately 260,434 km² of reef and 147,284 km² of mangrove. Their combined area is only a **two-family experimental denominator**, not all nature.

A real denominator also requires harmonized reference boundaries and a reviewed exclusive spatial partition or explicit overlap allocation. The sum of these two source areas has not established a spatial union. Within-case cohort checks cannot prove that the separate reference maps never overlap.

If both have a hypothetical 1,000 km² of otherwise comparable condition-equivalent loss, the selected area rule gives them the same index. Equal-family weighting gives the mangrove case approximately **1.768 times** as much influence per lost square kilometre because the mapped mangrove family is smaller. Neither index is a measured current TULIP Score.

The earlier synthetic 10% reef condition-loss and 15% mangrove condition-loss cases therefore rank by their condition-equivalent areas under the selected rule. Their ordering is conditional on comparability; it is not a scientific finding about which real ecosystem damage is worse.

The [reproducible decision analysis](../../scripts/analyze-tulip-nature-area-policy-selection.mjs) checks the algebra against all five existing cases, the same-area cross-family example and a disjoint subtype refinement. Its [report](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/nature-area-policy-selection-analysis.json) retains both policies and the changed ordering rather than hiding the alternative.

## What this settles and what it leaves

The default weighting principle is now explicit. New nodes cannot choose their own family weights, and subtype splits cannot add influence. Extents and the ecosystem universe stay fixed within a version; unrelated catalogue additions do not change them.

The full reference universe, reviewed compatible condition scales, numerical nature anchors and current node attribution remain unfinished. A missing family cannot be treated as unaffected. The two-family denominator cannot be extended silently to a global rating. This decision supplies one calibration policy choice, not approval to publish new numbers.
