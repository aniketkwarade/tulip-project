# Acceptance contract for a durable TULIP Score

Prepared 27 September 2026. This is a proposed **internal release gate**, not an approved scoring method. Current public decimal scores remain unchanged.

## What must be fixed before another curve is chosen

The replacement candidate represents **aggregate current attributable consequence burden**, separately to people and nature. A complete fixed-average composite retains 50/50 weights. A domain-only endpoint is a separate diagnostic, not a comparable composite; an exclusion cannot silently double the remaining weight. The [scope decision](./domain-combination-scope-decision.md) records the user-confirmed fixed 50/50 average and its compensation meaning, including 10-and-1 giving 5.5. This is an explicit TULIP construct decision, not a claim that every type of harm has a natural shared unit. A very severe local case can have less aggregate harm than a widespread milder case; its local severity must remain visible in the explanation, not receive an undeclared bonus in the score. The [hypothetical human-burden stress test](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/human-burden-stress.json) demonstrates that the current geometric candidate can reverse an illustrative person × duration × disability-weight ordering. The candidate therefore fails this construct and remains only a diagnostic comparator. The invented weights in the stress test are not empirical disability weights or calibration anchors.

The [anchor-saturation stress test](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/anchor-saturation.json) isolates a future-node problem: the experimental reach level caps at 100 million harmed people and human duration caps at ten years. With the same mild harm and two-week duration, 100 million and one billion harmed people both score **4.2** despite a tenfold burden difference in the illustrative calculation. Ten and forty years of the same harm also tie at **6.0**. Because the inputs have already become identical, no power or logarithmic transform applied *after* the ordinal product can separate them. If the construct is total current harm, the next candidate must preserve source-native magnitude farther into the decision range before mapping it onto 1–10. A bounded display still has finite resolution; the failure here is exact premature saturation below its maximum.

The [structured-route identifiability test](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/structured-identifiability.json) holds a source packet and all scope and rationale fields constant, changes only three entered ordinal levels, and obtains **3.0** and **10.0** from the permissive geometric diagnostic. Thus a plausible written rationale is not an executable classification rule. A newer evidence-bound gate rejects both as numerical node candidates. The structured fallback still lacks a calibrated derivation rule; rejection prevents a false pass but does not complete numerical coverage for future nodes.

The direct route had a narrower version of the same problem: the WHO modeled death interval fixed affected reach but did not originally constrain entered seriousness or duration. The [direct-mortality diagnostic](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/direct-mortality-identifiability.json) now passes that *specific* ambiguity check. The experimental evaluator requires a mortality-only endpoint scope, derives harmed-person reach from the exact source interval, and applies the existing TULIP choice that an attributable death has maximum seriousness and irreversible duration. A mortality count still omits nonfatal burden and does not validate a broader node or the final 1–10 transformation. The declared mortality-only scope must be checked against the node definition by a reviewer; a typed assertion alone cannot prove it.

The [catalogue route inventory](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/route-coverage.json) shows the scale of the remaining input problem. All 354 issue nodes have an operational metric binding, but **none of the 518 applicable domain drafts has a defined typed consequence endpoint**. Existing causal roles are 23 terminal consequences, 173 hazards or states, 61 aggregate pressures and 28 emissions sources; 69 nodes have no declared role. A metric such as emissions, temperature or exposed population can be useful evidence without being the harm the score claims to compare. The [outcome-timebase audit](./outcome-timebase-audit.md) further shows that the four existing source-summary categories do not normalize those metrics into comparable harm. The unclassified cases need node-boundary review, not a guessed role from their names.

The [source-native health-burden pilot](./source-native-health-burden-pilot.md) identifies a WHO joint ambient-and-household attributable-DALY endpoint. A recency check found a newer, rounded **2021** estimate in WHO's 2026 World health statistics report than the **2019** row returned by the matching GHO API query. Both retain the magnitude of fatal plus nonfatal health loss without squeezing it into three capped ordinal levels. A source-scope validator and synthetic fixed-anchor mapper test the mechanical route from raw burden to a decimal, including wrong-unit, reporting-period and new-node invariance challenges. This remains an endpoint input only: full node coverage, nature applicability and real 1–10 anchors remain unresolved.

The [air-pollution future-node admission example](./future-node-admission-example-air-pollution.md) checks that boundary against a newer 2023 State of Global Air source. It finds a broader pollutant death total and pollutant-specific DALY results, but no verified joint fatal-plus-nonfatal source row matching the node's full declared scope. The example names the missing scope, overlap and extraction work rather than upgrading a partial endpoint to a whole-node rating.

The [human-health anchor candidate](./human-health-anchor-candidate.md) replaces the purely synthetic magnitude examples with a **proposed**, catalogue-independent ruler: 100,000, 1 million, 10 million and 100 million annual attributable DALYs mark the middle odd-numbered scores, and WHO's 2021 global all-cause estimate supplies a fixed outer reference. Ten checksum-pinned WHO cause rows test its meaning without becoming TULIP nodes. This is a TULIP policy proposal, not a WHO standard or a calibrated whole-node method. Doubling or halving middle thresholds changes some illustrative readings by 0.6 points, so independent anchor adjudication remains necessary.

The [displacement life-year route audit](./displacement-life-year-route-audit.md) tests a published extension beyond health. IDMC's welfare-life-year formula combines affected people, disruption duration and a declared wellbeing-reduction parameter. The general note's 0.042 parameter misses its Bangladesh application's five displayed event examples; a published 0.046 upper mild-anxiety bound is consistent with all five at illustrative display precision. The application's exact parameter and unrounded inputs are not printed, so TULIP still needs to confirm the transfer rule before numerical use. Disaster-displacement movement flows, unique-person stocks and climate-attributed welfare loss remain separate measures.

The same pilot also tests a converted route for an emissions or activity source: an explicit modeled *fraction of attributable health burden* can be applied to the parent outcome when its source, counterfactual, geography, period and joint scenarios match. The test deliberately rejects a raw emissions share as a harm fraction. Its allocation values are synthetic; no source-specific causal model has passed review, so this does not yet score a real driver node.

The [source-native ecosystem-extent pilot](./source-native-ecosystem-extent-pilot.md) binds FAO's 2015–2025 gross deforestation rate to its exact forest-conversion boundary. It rejects a substitution of net forest loss or generic tree-cover loss. The physical extent is a useful nature-side input but cannot be treated as a calibrated nature-harm component without condition, persistence and overlap evidence. This experimental input does not change the catalogue's zero completed typed *whole-node* assessments.

The [nature common-unit feasibility check](./nature-common-unit-feasibility.md) tests a published biodiversity-risk unit against that forest input. LC-IMPACT V2 contains location- and land-use-specific species-loss-over-time factors, but FAO's global rate lacks the spatial and origin-to-destination detail needed to use them. For the same named forest-origin flow, selected country factors differ by thousands of times. More fundamentally, the model's PDF·year unit describes modeled global extinction risk, not all current ecosystem harm, and its authors do not prescribe a universal terrestrial/freshwater/marine weighting. This rules out silently treating area, coral cover or model PDF·year as interchangeable nature scores.

The same feasibility check now includes a source-bound GCRMN coral-condition endpoint. The validator reproduces the 1980–2009 versus 2020–2024 live-cover change and rejects altered units, periods, values and unsupported node attribution. Reusing that source observation for a future node yields the same condition statistic, but still no TULIP Score: one measured condition trend is not an attributable, calibrated whole-node harm assessment.

A [ten-region spatial audit](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/gcrmn-reef-cover-area-audit.json) now joins report-rounded subregional cover change to its mapped reef area. It finds paired cover for 47/51 subregions, yet the report supports decline classification for only 18; 46.92% of mapped area has no supported change classification. This supplies a source-matched cover-equivalent magnitude example while demonstrating why a global average, gross decline, and model point estimates without change evidence are not interchangeable. The audit assigns no attributable reef burden or nature score.

## Pass conditions for the method

1. **Define the target and boundary.** For each issue, declare the aggregate current attributable terminal consequences being compared with an explicit counterfactual. Fix the population or ecosystem distribution, geography, evidence period, stock-versus-flow normalization, and treatment of overlapping pathways. Do not add an undeclared premium for local intensity or future danger. The 2027–2036 outlook remains separate.
2. **Freeze absolute reference cases.** Specify what 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 10 mean for people and nature with real, independently source-checked consequence cases and stated denominators. Publish the conversion and interpolation rules. The current hypothetical anchors are useful design examples, not evidence that health, service loss and ecosystem loss are equivalent. New catalogue members must never change these anchors or the scores of existing members.
3. **Use typed, reproducible inputs.** Where possible, record source-native quantities and units before calculating components: for example attributable deaths and health loss, people and duration of an essential-service interruption, or ecosystem condition loss over a declared distribution and recovery period. Bind each quoted observation to a separately versioned source transcription with a precise locator and immutable artifact digest; the assessment packet cannot establish its own "source" numbers. The same endpoint, harmed group, place and time must support severity, reach and duration. Exposure, physical anomaly, source count or graph prominence is not a harm substitute. A human-only measured endpoint cannot silently exclude nature for a dual-domain driver.
4. **Give every evidence route a rule.** Direct observations need source-row matching. Converted estimates need an explicit equation, denominator and error range. A structured estimate needs predeclared measurable criteria or an adjudicated reference profile for *each* axis; free-text justification alone fails. A family analogue needs a specified transfer rule and node-specific adjustment, with a test showing that materially different evidence changes the output. A driver or emission source needs a causal allocation to terminal consequences rather than its aggregate node's score.
5. **Separate value choices from facts.** The 50/50 people/nature emphasis, any trade-off among harm types, ecological reference states and 1–10 conversion are TULIP policy choices. Source observations, model outputs and uncertainty are evidence. Store both with method and source versions so an assessor cannot silently retune weights to make a familiar node look right.
6. **Challenge the frozen method prospectively.** Before seeing desired scores, classify held-out real cases, the original 20 pilots, mixed severe/local versus mild/widespread cases, modeled routes, and new-node examples absent from the calibration set. Two internal reviewers independently reproduce source extraction and classification; log disagreements and adjudication. Check unit changes, new sources, plausible alternative models, uncertainty bounds, duplicate pathways and response-node exclusion. Test that adding an unrelated node changes no existing result and that larger harm with every other input fixed never lowers the score. Publish the failures as well as passes.
7. **Make numerical coverage honest.** Every *published* issue may display a one-decimal number only after it meets a declared minimum evidence or transferable-analogue standard. A provisional estimate may have broad bounds and plainly stated confidence. With no relevant consequence evidence or defensible analogue, no formula can produce an authentic harm rating; the node needs research before numerical publication. This is an admission rule for future nodes, not a request to remove today's public scores during method development. Responses remain outside danger scoring.

## The future-node admission test

The [counterfactual source-binding correction](./counterfactual-source-binding.md) enforces one part of the typed-input requirement: the source registry establishes the baseline identity, version and digest. A free-text baseline or a simultaneously relabeled packet and copied observation cannot reinterpret an unchanged quantity. The qualitative identity check does not substitute for reproducing the model's numeric exposure parameters.

Keep **method acceptance** separate from **node admission**. Passing tests on today's catalogue does not prove that an arbitrary future node has enough evidence. Conversely, a future node must not cause the conversion scale to be recalculated. The same test applies whenever an existing node receives new evidence.

For each proposed issue node, retain a versioned assessment packet with its definition and causal role; current attributable outcome or defensible transfer analogue; counterfactual; people/nature applicability; source-native units, numerator, denominator, place and period; source and model versions; overlap exclusions; lower/central/upper evidence cases where supported; and the exact method/anchor version. Mark each applicable component as **direct**, **modeled**, **analogue**, or **unsupported**. An unsupported component must never be treated as zero or silently removed from the 50/50 combination. A response is explicitly excluded from danger scoring.

The numerical admission decision is binary: the packet has a supported route for *every applicable component* and the frozen method yields a reproducible one-decimal value, or the node needs further research before a numerical rating is published. A broad but defensible modeled or analogue interval can still qualify if its assumptions and node-specific adjustment are recorded. A bare physical indicator, generic family score, free-text rationale or exposure count cannot qualify on its own. This rule makes future numerical coverage a research and publication responsibility, not a property falsely guaranteed by a formula.

Run the following falsification checks on every method version and on representative new-node packets:

| Check | Pass result |
| --- | --- |
| Catalogue independence | Add, remove or reorder an unrelated node; all unchanged evidence packets retain exactly the same unrounded and displayed values. No percentile or rank of the current catalogue enters normalization. |
| Ecosystem-type partition invariance | Splitting one ecosystem type into equivalent subtypes with the same area and condition cannot double its burden or alter an unrelated node. Freeze a typology version and specify how parent weights pass to children; a classification revision is a new method version. |
| Unit and boundary invariance | Equivalent unit conversions preserve the result; incompatible units, geography, period, population or counterfactual are rejected rather than coerced. |
| Source-record integrity | Verify the consumed record keys and joins, including row counts, conflicting measurements and identity fields. A checksum or successful file-integrity check cannot substitute for correct person/place/period linkage. Missing unrelated lookup fields are assessed separately from conflicts in the admitted route. |
| Harm responsiveness | With all other evidence fixed, more attributable harm cannot lower the component or overall value. Materially different burdens must remain distinguishable before display rounding unless the declared 10.0 ceiling is reached. |
| Route reproducibility | Two internal reviewers extracting the same source rows and applying the written rules obtain identical calculated values. Any judgmental transfer is logged and adjudicated before release. |
| Attribution and overlap | A driver receives only its modeled contribution to terminal consequences. Shared pathways and multiple source rows describing the same harm are counted once. |
| Ecological outcome coverage | A reviewed node profile identifies material ecological characteristics before selecting indicators. A net cover total cannot erase relevant community changes; the total and its constituent covers cannot be added as independent harms. Category refinements cannot create burden, and historical change is not silently substituted for current attribution. |
| Robustness | Held-out real cases and deliberately difficult mixed cases retain defensible interpretations when weights, anchors, source bounds and plausible model choices are varied. Large rank changes are disclosed and investigated, not hidden by a preferred curve. |
| Version stability | A new node uses the frozen method. Changing weights, anchors, endpoint definitions or transfer rules increments the method version and reruns the complete catalogue and held-out suite. |

The pass evidence is the assessment packet, executable checks, reviewer log and a before/after catalogue report. It is not the mere existence of a decimal score.

An acceptance report must list the exact method version, anchor cases, held-out cases, score and uncertainty for every issue, all review disagreements, sources and unresolved exceptions. A change to the construct, anchor, weight, transfer rule or curve is a new method version and requires rerunning the full catalogue and held-out tests. Scientific validation of a composite index also requires checking normalization, weights and ranking sensitivity; the [OECD/JRC handbook](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/handbook-on-constructing-composite-indicators-methodology-and-user-guide_9789264043466-en.html) is a useful external methodological reference, not an endorsement of TULIP's choices.

## Current status against this contract

The [executable fixed-benchmark calculator](./benchmark-score-kernel.md) implements the shared mapping/combination sequence with frozen input registries, exact policy-definition binding, pinned source versions, canonical units and final-only rounding. Independent Decimal arithmetic checks its exported cases. Its nature inputs are synthetic and its real WHO input is endpoint-only, so these mechanical passes do not admit whole nodes or approve nature calibration.

The [nature weighting decision](./nature-aggregation-selection.md) now records fixed physical area as the provisional default for comparable condition loss. This avoids giving extra influence per unit area solely because a mapped family is smaller; it does not establish a common ecological condition unit or replace the missing global reference universe.

The geometric experiment has useful scope and joint-scenario checks, and one mortality-only direct endpoint now has a deterministic source-to-axis rule. Its ordinal product is **rejected as the replacement aggregate-harm rule** because it loses material magnitude and can reverse compatible burden ordering. Source-native consequence quantities and absolute anchor mapping remain experimental; they do not yet cover whole nodes. The [catalogue ledger](../../artifacts/tulip-consequence-rule-audit-2026-09-27/review-ledger.json) contains 354 issue drafts and 27 response exclusions, but no completed typed *whole-node* consequence assessment. The two central and 110 lower-bound failures are identified in the [mixed-case decision](./mixed-case-calibration-decision.md). Passing schema tests or producing 354 decimal outputs cannot by itself establish that the scores represent measured harm.

The [nature-vector experiment](../../src/tulip-nature-burden-experiment.js) passes a narrow future-node stability test: fixed source-pinned reef and mangrove extent denominators, disjoint-cohort partition invariance, catalogue-addition invariance and within-type monotonicity. It deliberately returns no cross-type sum or numerical rating. This isolates the missing rule: reviewed, node-attributable condition loss and an explicit, independently challenged trade-off between unlike ecosystems. Its synthetic tests cannot count as completed nature components or satisfy whole-catalogue admission.

The [recent reef-event observation](../../src/tulip-reef-event-observation.js) supplies a real source-bound 2023–2024 modeled coral-cover decline, but explicitly marks its node-attributable fraction and paired-change uncertainty as unresolved. It is a useful current biological outcome input, not a passed `coral_bleaching` score. A future node can reuse its source protocol without changing the observation, while needing its own causal boundary and admitted consequence route.

The [ecosystem severity normalization candidate](./ecosystem-severity-normalization-candidate.md) adds a reproducible continuous-indicator rule based on a named reference and collapse threshold, with matching current with-node/without-node cases required for an incremental input. It checks unit/orientation and new-node invariance and rejects incompatible scenario fields. Three IUCN worked input cases are extracted; one printed percentage discrepancy is retained. This is progress on the evidence-to-severity rule, not approved whole-node nature calibration or a current-harm interpretation of IUCN risk categories.

The [fixed nature-aggregation experiment](./nature-aggregation-policy-challenge.md) corrects the type-count and subtype-split defects on a deliberately incomplete two-family test universe. The weights and reference denominators are separate from the node packet; missing family coverage is not reweighted away. Nineteen adversarial cases pass, and a separate arithmetic check reproduces ten synthetic policy outputs. Equal-family and fixed-area weights reverse a cross-type pair, leaving the ecological exchange rate uncalibrated. This establishes an invariant architecture to challenge with real cases, not a global nature method or catalogue admission.

The [real marine-reference audit](./marine-foundation-real-case-calibration-audit.md) imports 94 historical regional summaries representing 1,322 sampled area/event observations from two checksum-pinned workbooks. Five endpoint types are kept separate. A real average-gain case still contains loss observations, prompting rejection of net-mean substitution for loss-preserving spatial cohorts. The pool is useful calibration evidence but has no completed current node assessments, inferred damaged areas or invented probability bounds.

The [HeatCRD input-quality audit](./heatcrd-calibration-input-quality-audit.md) adds 2,078 historical, matched cover-change references across 719 sites. It detects incompatible base-table ID linkages despite successful SQLite file integrity, and quarantines conflicting sample measurements. The self-contained query snapshots are internally consistent on consumed fields. This establishes a source-lineage admission requirement and a real within-indicator reference pool, not current causal attribution, damaged habitat area or whole-node calibration.

The [ecological outcome coverage rule](./ecological-outcome-coverage-rule.md) adds 262 historical coral community vectors from a separate source. Three sites have unchanged total coral cover despite category changes; positive category-difference sums also depend on taxonomic partition and cannot be treated as invariant harm units. The audit preserves 140 changed mean-depth pairs as potential confounders and keeps a supplement/raw-export mean discrepancy unresolved. These real cases require material-outcome coverage and overlapping-measurement review before any cover-based nature calibration can stand for a whole node.
